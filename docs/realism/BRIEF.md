# Brief: make the 3D line look like a real machine, not a toy

## Goal

The three.js view of the pen-assembly line (see README.md, `src/view`, `tools/blender/parts.py`)
reads as "Playmobil": primary-colour primitives on an empty plane. Make it look like a real
industrial machine. Not photorealism: the target is a **convincing industrial digital-twin render,
like a machine builder's marketing visualisation** (7–8 on the rubric in `docs/realism/RUBRIC.md`).

Baseline: three independent judges (GPT-6.2 Sol, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2) scored the
current look **4/10** (per-view 3–5.5). Their full verdicts are in `docs/realism/scores-before.md`;
read them, they are specific. **Bar for "done"**: blind re-scoring by the same judges averages
**≥ 7/10**, every view gains **≥ 2 points**, and nothing in their "Broken" lists remains.

## What the judges agree on (priority order)

1. **Environment**: the line stands on an infinite grey plane (the "table", `line.ts`) with a black
   void / hard horizon above it in every station camera. Build a factory hall: epoxy/concrete floor
   with joints, safety-yellow aisle lines, walls/columns, a ceiling with strip lights, and an
   industrial-looking environment map for reflections (e.g. a procedurally built or CC0 HDRI).
2. **Materials & lighting**: everything is one matte grey. Differentiate anodised aluminium,
   brushed stainless, painted steel (machine bases), black POM/plastics, rubber, polycarbonate.
   Contact shadows / ambient occlusion, softer and higher-quality shadows (no stair-stepping).
3. **De-toy the colours**: saturated red bins and blue gantry beams. Use real-world RAL-ish tones
   (light grey RAL 7035 machine bases, anodised natural aluminium, muted signal colours only where
   functional: e.g. a red REJECT label on a grey/blue Euro container with rims and ribs).
4. **Structure**: plain square posts → aluminium T-slot extrusion profiles (40/45 mm, slots via
   geometry or normal map, end caps, corner brackets, levelling feet). Conveyor needs supports/legs,
   slotted side profiles, a drive motor/gearbox at a return, belt texture. Machines should stand on
   real machine bases/tables with legs, not on an infinite plane.
5. **Mechanisms**: pick-and-place → linear axes with rails, carriages, servo motors, cable/drag
   chains, a real gripper. Presses → C-frame / cylinder body with a round ram. Cameras → lens barrel,
   ring light, bracket, cable. Dial → indexer housing, recessed nests. Bowl feeders → spiral track
   and drive base, legible handoff into the linear feeder rail. Gate stoppers → real pneumatic
   stopper units (not loose yellow cylinders).
6. **Guarding and machine furniture**: polycarbonate guards on extrusion frames (keep the work
   visible through them, and keep the station cameras' views clear), control cabinet, HMI, e-stops,
   stack lights on proper poles, cable trunking, pneumatic tubing, valve islands.
7. **Parts**: movers (pucks) look like toy modules (pale yellow plates, green domes); pens/housings
   could use clear-coat / printed detail; trays and totes need rims and definition.
8. **Fix broken bits** listed by the judges: z-fighting red panel/chute, gantry leg through a bin,
   floating red block at the tray table, stoppers clipping pucks, rams passing through crossbeams,
   stray dark band on the overview, aliased shadows, black planks/teal dots at the presses.

## Hard constraints

- **Keep behaviour and data**: `src/sim` is not hands-off, but only change it where it supports
  realism (e.g. a layout dimension); keep `npm test` and `npm run typecheck` green.
- **Station cameras are fixed** (`src/view/cameras.ts`): vision work depends on them. Don't move
  them, don't block their view of the movers, the mover side number labels and the pen DataMatrix
  (both must stay legible in the station cameras). Guards in front of a camera must be clear and
  not hide the parts.
- **Object oracle** (`src/view/oracle.ts`, `userData.track`): keep tracked objects tracked, and
  don't add big new occluders between cameras and tracked objects.
- **Performance**: the server streams up to 6 camera feeds from one headless Chrome on the same
  GPU (`src/server/cameras.ts`, `?capture=1`), at 25 fps each, plus the browser view. Keep the frame
  cost reasonable: prefer baked detail (textures, normal maps, baked AO, instancing, merged
  geometry) over heavy per-frame post-processing. If you add SSAO/SSR-style passes, make them
  cheap or limit them to the interactive view, and measure: report ms/frame before and after for
  the overview and one station camera (e.g. via a quick puppeteer timing of the render loop).
- **Camera realism** (`src/view/camerafx.ts`, `?fx=1`) and the lighting presets (day/evening/
  night/dim in `scene.ts`) must keep working; the hall should look right in all four.
- **Assets**: 3D models are generated by Blender scripts (`tools/blender/*.py`, Blender 5.3 at
  `/opt/homebrew/bin/blender`) so they're reproducible; keep that approach for new models (more
  scripts are fine). Textures may be procedural (canvas/Blender bakes) or CC0 downloads
  (Poly Haven, ambientCG) at ≤ 2K, recorded in `public/ASSETS.md` with source URL and licence.
  Total new assets under ~25 MB. Nothing that needs network access at runtime.
- **Don't commit**; the coordinator reviews and commits. Don't touch `docs/realism/before/`.

## How to iterate

`node tools/scripts/shots.mjs <dir>` starts the dev server on port 5199 (fixed seeds) and renders
the 11 views (`--only overview,hero-robot` for a subset, `--lighting night` for another preset).
Look at your shots after each major change and compare with `docs/realism/before/`. When done,
render the final set to `docs/realism/after/` (day) plus `docs/realism/after-night/overview.png`
and `docs/realism/after-night/infeed.png` (`--lighting night`).

Don't run anything else on the GPU while `shots.mjs` runs, and don't run two at once.
